1 Executive Summary & Problem Scope

Over 4.5 million pilgrims navigate Uttarakhand's steep, fragile mountain corridors every monsoon season. The region is afflicted by compounding high-velocity hazards: devastating landslides, localized cloudbursts, Glacial Lake Outburst Floods (GLOF), narrow-path crowd congestion crushes, and the rapid virality of unverified disaster rumors that incite panic evacuations.

CrisisGuard AI provides the first unified operational picture for the Himalayan pilgrimage ecosystem. It fuses live radar sweeps from the India Meteorological Department (IMD), landslide susceptibility data from the Geological Survey of India (GSI BhuSanket), high-altitude Automatic Weather Stations (AWS), and dynamic pilgrim crowd metrics into an actionable, multi-persona response engine.

⚡ Key Innovation: Dual National Authority Alignment

Unlike isolated weather apps or static disaster portals, CrisisGuard synchronously correlates Atmospheric Triggers from IMD MoES (Doppler Radar reflectivity >45 dBZ & 3-hour nowcasts) with Geological Ground Conditions from GSI BhuSanket (Factor of Safety, slope angle >35°, soil saturation) to eliminate blind spots across NH-07 and NH-107.

3 Detailed Specifications of the 5 Core Hazard Engines

Engine Core Algorithm & Trigger Science Official Data Feeds Mitigation & Response Output
1. Landslide Risk Engine Calculates dynamic Factor of Safety (FoS) using slope geometry, geomorphological shear strength, and cumulative 72-hour antecedent rainfall saturation (>120 mm). GSI BhuSanket LEWS REST Servers, IMD Rainfall Thresholds, Rudraprayag Pilot Basin. Km-wise highway hazard categorization on NH-07 (Km 282 Lambagar Chute) & NH-107. Triggers SDRF escorted single-lane convoys.
2. Cloudburst Nowcast Engine Detects sudden localized convective cloud spikes (>50 mm/hr) within narrow Himalayan river valleys (Mandakini and Alaknanda catchments). IMD Dehradun DWR Radar (/radar_image), IMD Nowcast (/nowcast_district), Kedarnath AWS. 3-hour localized advance warning window. Automated siren alarms in riverside transit camps; immediate trek halts.
3. GLOF Surveillance Engine Monitors high-altitude proglacial lake expansion rate, moraine dam freeboard loss, and seismic breach vulnerability. Sentinel-2 SAR imagery, Wadia Institute (WIHG) archives, Downstream River Telemetry. Downstream flood transit lead times: Sonprayag (45 min), Gaurikund (18 min). Automatic holding camp evacuations.
4. Crowd Chokepoint Engine Tracks pilgrim accumulation rates exceeding safe capacity (>4 persons/m²) on narrow mountain trails and mule staging hubs. UTDB RFID Yatra Cards, Mobile Tower Density, Drone / CCTV Count Feeds. Regulated batch releases from Guptkashi holding camps. Real-time trek queue wait times (e.g., 4.5h wait at Gaurikund).
5. Fake News Buster Engine NLP-powered cross-verification of viral social media disaster claims against verified USDMA and SDRF incident dispatch logs. USDMA State Control Room, SDRF Quick Response Log, Social Media API Scrapers. Official Credibility Score (0-100%) and public debunking bulletins to prevent deadly mass panic and stampedes.

6 Official Emergency Action Protocol Matrix

Alert Tier Official Color Scientific Conditions Triggered Enacted Operational Mandate
Level 1: Normal GREEN Rainfall <15 mm/day, soil saturation <40%, clear radar echoes, crowd capacity <70%. Normal pilgrimage transit. Standard SDRF highway patrol operating. Regular advisory notices.
Level 2: Watch YELLOW Rainfall 15-64 mm/day, gusty winds 30-40 km/h, radar echoes 30-40 dBZ, crowd capacity 70-90%. Advisory broadcast to pilgrim apps. Staging earthmovers near Lambagar & Sirobagarh choke points.
Level 3: Alert ORANGE Very heavy rain (65-115 mm/day), high landslide vulnerability, radar echoes 40-48 dBZ, crowd capacity 90-110%. Single-lane escorted convoys on NH-07/NH-107. Night transit halted. Regulated batch release at Sonprayag.
Level 4: Warning RED Extremely heavy rain (>115 mm/day), cloudburst detected (>50 mm/h, >48 dBZ), GLOF watch, crowd overload >110%. Immediate suspension of yatra. Evacuation of riverside camps to designated safe shelters. Full SDRF/NDRF mobilization.

7 Hackathon Impact & Future Deployment Roadmap

  • Zero-Latency Edge Execution: High-performance client-side GIS rendering using Leaflet and GeoJSON ensures smooth 60fps interaction even on low-cost Android devices used by field staff.
  • Offline-First Himalayan Resilience: When mountain fiber cables sever or cellular towers overload, cached PWA blueprints ensure uninterrupted navigation to verified safe shelters.
  • Hardware Expansion (Phase 2): Integration of solar-powered LoRaWAN mesh communication beacons along the 16km Gaurikund-to-Kedarnath pedestrian trek for zero-cell-coverage SOS alerts.
  • Automated AI Drone Reconnaissance: Direct API hooks for autonomous drone camera inspection of inaccessible moraine dams in the Mandakini and Alaknanda headwaters.
